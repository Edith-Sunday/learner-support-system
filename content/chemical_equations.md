# Chemical Equations—From Formulae to Balancing

Adapted from selected SS1 Week 5–6 topics. Only this module is implemented.

## Reactants and products

Reactants are the starting substances, on the left of →. Products are the substances formed, on the right. The arrow means “react to form”. A plus sign separates different substances.

## Reading formulae and counting atoms

A chemical symbol names an element: H is hydrogen, O is oxygen, Fe is iron. A small number after a symbol (a subscript) counts its atoms in one molecule or formula unit. No subscript means 1.

H₂O has 2 hydrogen atoms and 1 oxygen atom. The coefficient before a formula multiplies **every** atom in that formula:

| Expression | Hydrogen atoms | Oxygen atoms |
|---|---:|---:|
| H₂O | 2 | 1 |
| 2H₂O | 4 | 2 |
| 3H₂O | 6 | 3 |

## Conservation of atoms

In a chemical reaction, atoms are rearranged, not created or destroyed. A balanced equation has the same number of **each element's atoms** on both sides. The number of molecules need not be the same.

Change coefficients to balance an equation. **Never change the formulae.** Changing H₂O to H₂O₂ changes water into hydrogen peroxide, a different substance. Use the smallest positive whole-number coefficients. Include 1 when entering your answers.

## Worked example: hydrogen and oxygen

Start with H₂ + O₂ → H₂O.

1. Count: left H = 2, O = 2; right H = 2, O = 1.
2. Put 2 before H₂O: H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. Now the right has H = 4 and O = 2. Oxygen matches, but hydrogen does not.
3. Put 2 before H₂: **2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O**.
4. Check: left H = 2×2 = 4, O = 1×2 = 2; right H = 2×2 = 4, O = 2×1 = 2.

The coefficients are **2, 1, 2**. They share no common factor above 1, so they are the smallest whole numbers.

## Worked example: odd and even counts

Start with Fe + O₂ → Fe₂O₃.

1. Oxygen comes in pairs on the left (2) and groups of 3 on the right. The smallest common total is 6.
2. Put 3 before O₂ and 2 before Fe₂O₃: Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃. Oxygen: left 3×2 = 6; right 2×3 = 6.
3. The right now has 2×2 = 4 iron atoms. Put 4 before Fe.
4. **4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃**. Check Fe: 4 = 4. Check O: 6 = 6.

The coefficients **4, 3, 2** are the smallest positive whole numbers. The odd oxygen subscript 3 becomes an even total by choosing coefficient 2; we did not change the subscript.

## A method you can reuse

List each element. Count atoms on both sides. Adjust coefficients, counting again after every change. Check all elements and divide by any common factor. For combustion, try carbon first, hydrogen next, oxygen last.
